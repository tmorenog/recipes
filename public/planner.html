Build the Meal Planner

An agent that takes a budget, reads the recipes the class has found, and plans five dinners, Monday to Friday, that are nutritious, varied and cheap. It shows the week and saves the plan to the coordinator.

The goal

Your Planner is done when: you enter a budget, press a button, watch it work step by step, and a Monday-to-Friday plan appears in your app and on the Database page under your group.

  • It uses only recipes the class saved, with the prices the Pricer worked out.
  • It estimates each dinner’s nutrition from the ingredients, using USDA data from the coordinator.
  • It learns the rules from the coordinator (get_expectations) instead of having them written into its code.
  • It checks its plan with the coordinator, and improves it until the rules pass or it can explain why they can’t.
  • Costs are added up by the coordinator, never guessed by the AI.
  • You can see every step it took.

Build it in Lovable, step by step

Each step says what it adds, then gives a sample prompt. Copy the prompt into Lovable as it is, or edit it to make the app your own, then check the result before moving on. Your app uses Lovable Cloud for its backend and Lovable’s built-in AI for the agent, so the only key you need is the class key.

Step 1 of 5Create the app

Sets up your Lovable project and the page: a budget, a box for anything else to consider, a button, and an empty Monday-to-Friday week. No agent yet.

Sample prompt

You should see: the page with the budget (15 to start), the text box, the button and five empty day cards.

Before step 2: your group name and the class key

Pick one name for your group and use it for every agent you build, e.g. team-3. It labels everything your agents save. The prompts below fill it in.

The class key never goes in a prompt. Step 2 asks Lovable to create a secret. Lovable then shows a secure box where you paste the key.

Step 2 of 5Connect to the coordinator

Connects your app to the class coordinator, which has the recipes with their prices, USDA nutrition data, and the rules for a good plan. No AI yet.

Sample prompt

You should see: five coordinator tools, the brief from get_expectations (the goal, the balance rules and the plan format), and a table of priced recipes. If the table is empty, the class hasn’t saved recipes yet, or the Pricer is still working on them.

Step 3 of 5Add the agent and show the week

Adds the AI. The model reads the recipes, estimates their nutrition, drafts five dinners, asks the coordinator to check them, and improves the plan. The week appears in your app, not saved yet.

Sample prompt

You should see: press the button, watch it list recipes and check drafts, and five dinners fill Monday to Friday, with the week’s cost against the budget and a ✓ or ✗ for each rule.

Step 4 of 5Save the plan to the coordinator

Lets the agent save its best plan to the class database, where everyone can see it.

Sample prompt

You should see: a run that ends with save_meal_plan, and the plan on the Database page under Meal plans.

Step 5 of 5Show the agent working

Makes the agent’s work easy to follow: a live trace with a counter, failed rules in red, and a history of past runs.

Sample prompt

You should see: a clear, live trace, and a list of past runs you can reopen.

Test it

It works when…

  • The trace starts with get_expectations
  • It calls check_meal_plan before saving, and fixes failed rules
  • A run with a $15 budget saves a plan, Monday to Friday
  • The plan appears on the Database page with its rule checks

Now try to break it

  • A tiny budget: $3 for the week. Does it explain that it can’t be done, or pretend?
  • A clash: “vegetarian only” with fewer than five vegetarian recipes. What does it choose?
  • A rule of your own: “no dinner over 45 minutes”. Does it follow it, and say so?
  • A tight limit: ask Lovable to set the limit to 6 tool calls. What does it do when it runs out?

Reference

How it works

Recipes reach your Planner in two steps. The Scouts save recipes to the coordinator. Then the Pricer, an agent that runs on the coordinator, prices each one at Kroger. Your Planner estimates nutrition, chooses and explains; the coordinator adds up costs and checks the rules.

{{SITE}}/api/mcp?agent=planner offers the Planner five tools. Every request carries Authorization: Bearer <class key> and X-Group: {{GROUP}}.

ToolDoes
get_expectationsThe goal, the steps, the balance rules and the plan format
list_recipesThe class’s recipes, with their ingredients. With {"priced": true}, only priced ones, each with pricing.cost_per_serving_usd
search_foodsUSDA nutrition per 100 g for a plain food, e.g. “chickpeas canned”, to estimate each dinner’s nutrition
check_meal_planChecks a draft without saving: the week’s cost per person, and every rule with ✓ or ✗
save_meal_planSaves the plan with its numbers and rule checks
The balance rules (your agent reads these from get_expectations)
  • The week costs no more than the budget, per person
  • Every dinner has 400 to 800 calories, at least 20 g protein, at least 5 g fibre and less than 1,500 mg sodium per serving
  • At least 3 different cuisines
  • No category (e.g. Beef, Chicken) more than 2 times
  • At least one vegetarian or vegan dinner

Classroom rules, not dietary advice. Nutrition is your agent’s estimate, grounded in USDA data from search_foods. Discuss as a group whether you’d change them; the model’s instructions can add rules of your own.

The meal plan format
FieldWhat goes in it
budget_usdThe budget: US dollars per person for the whole week
summaryHow the five dinners balance nutrition, variety and cost, and any trade-offs
mealsExactly 5: day (Monday to Friday, each once), recipe_id (a priced recipe from list_recipes, each once), why (one sentence) and nutrition_per_serving (your estimate: calories, protein_g, fiber_g, sodium_mg)

That’s all the agent sends. The coordinator adds each dinner’s cost from the Pricer, the week’s total, and the rule checks. A plan that breaks a balance rule is still saved, with the rule marked ✗.

When things go wrong

“isn’t priced yet”The Pricer hasn’t finished that recipe. Ask Lovable to call list_recipes with {"priced": true}, or check the Pricer page.
No priced recipesThe Scouts haven’t saved any yet, or the Pricer is still working. Look at the Pricer page.
Nutrition numbers look made up“Before estimating nutrition, look up the main ingredients with search_foods and scale them to one serving.”
The agent adds up costs itself“Make sure the agent uses check_meal_plan for every total, and shows the coordinator’s numbers.”
A CORS error in the browser consoleThe page is calling the coordinator directly. “Move every call to the coordinator into a backend function.”
